## Design Context

### Users
Hiring managers, recruiters, freelance clients, and developer peers — all three audiences equally. They arrive to evaluate Navaneeth's technical capability and creative range, usually from a link on a resume, LinkedIn, or GitHub. They're scanning quickly and deciding whether to dig deeper within 30 seconds.

### Brand Personality
**Inventive, deliberate, multidisciplinary.** "I build things that think." This is someone with a Physics and Anthropology background who writes automation pipelines and AI tools — the portfolio should feel like it was made by someone who understands systems *and* people. Technical depth without the cold sterility. Creative range without the performative flair.

### Emotional Goal
Within 30 seconds: "This person has range — technical AND creative." The site itself should be proof of that claim, not just a container for case studies that argue it.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Tone**: Technical-editorial. Monospace body text conveys precision; rounded display font (Unbounded) adds warmth and personality. Confident, unhurried, information-dense.
- **Theme**: Light — warm cream paper (`oklch(0.97 0.005 80)`) with warm terracotta accent (`oklch(0.45 0.14 30)`).
- **Typography**: Unbounded (display, 300/400/700) — wide, authoritative, slightly strange. Doesn't read as "a developer portfolio font." Martian Mono (body, 300/400) — precision without the tired "I'm a programmer" cosplay of Space Mono.
- **Color**: Single warm terracotta accent against a tinted paper ground. Not teal. Not blue. Every neutral tinted toward the accent hue — you feel it even if you can't name it.
- **Layout**: Two-column grids (hero, about, contact). Three-column project rows (number | body | link). Explicit horizontal rules as section dividers.
- **Project numbers**: Large, structural, color of the rule lines — scaffolding, not decoration. On hover they flush to the accent. Text runs alongside, not below.
- **Status pill**: "Open to roles" with pulsing dot — recruiters notice it immediately.
- **Anti-references**: Generic Tailwind/shadcn dev portfolios, flashy Three.js/WebGL showoff sites, clean-but-boring resume-as-website layouts, Behance-style case study grids.
- **Color avoidance**: No teal. No blue. No AI-default cyan/purple/neon palettes.

### Design Principles
1. **The site is the portfolio** — the design quality of the site itself is evidence of creative range, not just a frame around case studies.
2. **Precision over decoration** — every visual choice should feel deliberate, not decorative. No filler elements.
3. **Show range through restraint** — demonstrate sophistication by what's left out, not what's piled on. The complexity lives in the projects; the portfolio should be the confident, quiet frame.
4. **Earn attention, don't demand it** — no gimmicks, no scroll-jacking, no loading animations. Let the content hierarchy and typographic craft do the work.
5. **Cross-audience legibility** — a recruiter scanning for keywords and a developer studying architecture choices should both find what they need within seconds.
